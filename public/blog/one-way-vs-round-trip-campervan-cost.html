One-Way vs Round-Trip Campervan Rental: What Each One Really Costs (Plus a Third Way Almost Nobody Uses)

Picking up a campervan in one city and dropping it off in another sounds simple — until you see the one-way fee. Most travellers end up choosing round-trip instead, driving back the way they came just to avoid it. But that "free" choice has its own hidden cost: wasted days and extra fuel. Here's the real cost of each option — and a third, almost never-advertised route that quietly undercuts them both.

Option 1: One-way rental (with the fee)

A standard one-way rental lets you pick up in city A and drop off in city B, no driving back required. Convenient — but rental companies charge for it, because someone has to move that vehicle back afterwards.

Typical one-way fees: €500–1500, depending on distance and how far the drop-off city is from the company's nearest depot. A short hop (Munich to Salzburg) might be a few hundred euros. A long cross-border route (Munich to Lisbon) can hit the top of that range, sometimes more.

Why the fee is so high: the fee isn't really about your trip — it's the company's cost of getting the van back to where it's needed, whether that's an empty repositioning drive, a transport truck, or paying another renter to do it. You're covering their logistics problem.

Which raises a question: what if you could be the "another renter" the company pays to do it — instead of the one who gets charged? More on that below.

Option 2: Round-trip rental (no fee, but you drive twice)

Round-trip avoids the fee entirely — you pick up and drop off in the same city. The catch: you have to drive back the way you came, which means every route becomes a there-and-back, not a one-way adventure.

What that actually costs you:

Time: If your outbound drive is 2 days, your return drive is another 2 days. On a 10-day trip, that's 4 days spent driving the same roads twice — 40% of your trip gone to retracing your route.

Fuel and tolls: Doubling the distance roughly doubles fuel and toll costs for that leg. On a longer route (say, 1,200km each way), that can easily add €150–300 in fuel alone compared to a one-way trip covering the same ground once.

Route flexibility: You can't really do an open-ended "start in Munich, end wherever" trip — you always have to plan your return leg, which limits spontaneity.

So neither option is great: pay a steep fee, or pay in days and diesel. There's a third way that sidesteps both problems — and it's the one rental companies rarely advertise.

Option 3: the one most people never hear about — relocation deals

Here's the thing both of the above options miss: rental companies already have to move vehicles one-way, constantly. A camper dropped off in Lisbon by one renter needs to get back to Munich for the next one. Someone has to drive that leg regardless.

Instead of paying a transport company or absorbing the cost themselves, providers offer that exact drive to travellers at a fraction of the price — often €1–5 per day instead of the €500-1500 one-way fee. You get a genuine one-way trip, no drive-back required, and the price undercuts round-trip rental too once you factor in the extra fuel and days round-trip costs you.

The trade-off: relocation deals only exist on routes and dates the company actually needs done. You can't request "Munich to Lisbon on the 14th" and expect it to exist — you search what's available and build your trip around it. That's the flexibility you give up in exchange for the price. Which option makes sense for you

Choose one-way rental if you need a specific route on specific dates and no relocation deal covers it. You'll pay the full fee, but you get exactly the trip you want.

Choose round-trip rental if you genuinely want to explore one region and don't mind retracing your steps — a round-trip through the Alps, for example, might make sense because the return drive is scenic in its own right, not wasted time.

Choose a relocation deal if you're flexible on exact dates and open to letting the available routes shape your trip. Frequently asked questions

How much does a one-way campervan rental fee cost?

One-way fees typically range from €500 to €1500 depending on the distance and how far the drop-off city is from the company's nearest depot. Long cross-border routes sit at the high end of that range.

Is round-trip campervan rental actually cheaper?

Round-trip avoids the one-way fee, but you pay for it in time and fuel: you have to drive back to your starting city, which can eat 2-4 days of your trip and add hundreds of euros in extra fuel and tolls.

What is a campervan relocation and how is it different?

A relocation deal is a one-way trip a rental company needs done anyway. Because the company benefits from the move, they charge €1-5/day instead of the full one-way fee.

When does a one-way rental make more sense than a relocation deal?

When you need a specific route on specific dates that no relocation deal covers. Relocations only exist on routes and dates companies actually need moved.
